THE
MUSEUM

Standing proudly in the heart of Manila's museum complex, the National Museum of Natural History is a testament to the Philippines' extraordinary biodiversity and geological heritage. Housed in a beaux-arts building crowned by a stunning geodesic dome, it is as much an architectural marvel as it is a scientific treasure trove.

Highlights

Exhibits Not to Miss

Architecture & Icon

The Tree of Life

The museum's crown jewel — a soaring sculptural tree rising through the central atrium beneath a geodesic glass dome that floods the space with natural light. A must-photograph moment.

Paleontology Hall

The Whale Skeleton

A colossal sperm whale skeleton suspended in the grand hallway — one of the most dramatic displays in the museum, framed by the building's ornate marble arches.

Biodiversity Wing

Marine Life Hall

Life-size models of manta rays and whale sharks hang overhead while preserved specimens in illuminated jars line the gallery floor — celebrating the Philippines' incredible ocean heritage.

Space Gallery

Orconuma Meteorite

A 4.6-billion-year-old piece of the asteroid belt, the Orconuma meteorite is an H3-4 ordinary chondrite — one of the oldest rocks you'll ever stand next to. Get up close with the magnifying glasses provided.

Marine Conservation

Hawksbill Turtle

A beautifully preserved specimen of the critically endangered Hawksbill Turtle — found in Philippine coral reefs and beaches. A gift of the Halili family to the National Museum.

Wildlife Gallery

Philippine Eagle

One of the world's largest and most powerful eagles — and the national bird of the Philippines. This taxidermy specimen captures the majestic scale and fierce presence of this critically endangered raptor.

Plan Your Visit

Visitor Tips

01

Admission is Free

Entry to the National Museum of Natural History is completely free for Filipino citizens and residents. Foreign nationals pay a minimal fee. No reservations needed.

02

Go Early

Arrive when doors open at 10 AM on weekdays to avoid weekend crowds. The atrium looks magical in the morning light streaming through the dome.

03

Wear Comfy Shoes

The museum spans multiple floors connected by wide staircases and the famous Tree of Life elevator. You'll cover a lot of ground — plan for 2–3 hours minimum.

04

Look Up. Always.

From the geodesic dome to the suspended whale skeleton and the hanging manta rays, the most spectacular sights are above your head. Don't forget to look up!

05

Museum Complex

The NMNH is one of three museums in the complex — the National Museum of Fine Arts and the National Museum of Anthropology are steps away. Make a full day of it.

06

Photography Welcome

Personal photography is allowed throughout. The Tree of Life atrium, meteorite gallery, and whale hall are especially photogenic — bring your best angles.

Security screening at the entrance — expect a short queue on weekends. Present one valid ID per group at Step #2.
